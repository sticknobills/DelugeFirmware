The Deluge can act as a USB audio interface. When it's connected to a computer over its USB type-B port, it shows up
as a class-compliant audio device alongside its existing USB MIDI ports, so no driver is needed. You can:

- play audio from your computer through the Deluge's outputs
- record the Deluge's output into your computer
- record and monitor audio from your computer in an audio clip, just like the line input

## Format

In the default stereo mode:

|             | From computer to Deluge | From Deluge to computer |
| ----------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------- |
| Channels    | 2 (stereo)              | 2 (stereo)              |
| Sample rate | 44.1 kHz                | 44.1 kHz                |
| Bit depth   | 24-bit                  | 24-bit                  |

The Deluge's own sample clock sets the rate for audio going to the computer. For audio coming from the computer, the
Deluge offers two modes, and the computer picks whichever it supports:

- **Following the Deluge's clock.** Computers that support this (such as macOS) send audio at exactly the Deluge's
  rate, so it's passed straight through.
- **Following the computer's clock.** Computers that can't follow the Deluge's clock, such as Windows, send at their
  own rate instead. The Deluge occasionally drops or repeats a single sample to stay in step, which isn't normally
  audible.

<Aside type="caution">Windows support hasn't been confirmed yet.</Aside>

## Setting up

1. Connect the Deluge to your computer with a USB cable.
2. Choose the Deluge as an audio input and/or output device in your computer's sound settings or your DAW. On macOS
   it's listed in Audio MIDI Setup.

The Deluge's USB MIDI ports keep working as before.

## Settings

USB audio's settings are in `SETTINGS > USB AUDIO` (`USBA` on 7SEG):

| Setting              | 7SEG   | Options                                                                     |
| -------------------- | ------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `MODE`               | `MODE` | `OFF`, `STEREO` (default, `STER`), `MULTITRACK` (`MULT`) - see below        |
| `INPUT`              | `IN`   | On (default), off - see below                                               |
| `OUTPUT`             | `OUT`  | `MAIN MIX` (default, `MIX`), `LINE IN` (`LINE`) - stereo mode only, below   |
| `DELAY COMPENSATION` | `CMP`  | 0-100 ms, 0 (off) by default - multitrack mode only, see below              |

## Modes

`SETTINGS > USB AUDIO > MODE` sets what the Deluge offers your computer:

- **`OFF`**: USB MIDI only, exactly as before USB audio was added.
- **`STEREO`** (the default): USB MIDI, and one stereo stream each way, as described on most of this page.
- **`MULTITRACK`**: USB MIDI, eight channels from the Deluge to the computer and four back, at 16 bits. You choose which
  tracks go to which channels. See [Multitrack mode](#multitrack-mode).

The setting takes effect the next time the Deluge is turned on, since your computer only finds out what kind of
device the Deluge is when it first connects. Multitrack mode needs some extra memory, set aside when the Deluge starts
up in that mode - none is used in the other modes.

<Aside>
  If you've used the Deluge over USB MIDI before, your computer may treat it as
  a new device the first time you connect it with this firmware, since it now
  describes itself differently. Unplugging and reconnecting it should sort out
  any problems with it not appearing.
</Aside>

## Playing audio from your computer

Audio from the computer is mixed straight into the Deluge's main outputs and headphones, after the song's master
volume and effects. This is on by default, and can be turned off with `SETTINGS > USB AUDIO > INPUT`. When it's off,
you'll only hear the computer's audio through an audio clip that's monitoring it, or while sampling it.

This audio is **not** sent back to the computer as part of the Deluge's output, so you won't get an echo. To put the
computer's audio through the Deluge's effects, monitor it with an audio clip instead (see below).

When an audio clip is monitoring the USB input (see below), the computer's audio is heard through that track instead,
with its volume and effects. The Deluge stops mixing it straight to the outputs while that's happening, so you never
hear it twice. Mixing straight to the outputs comes back by itself once nothing's monitoring the USB input, for example
when a sampler clip has finished recording.

## Recording the Deluge into your computer

`SETTINGS > USB AUDIO > OUTPUT` sets what the computer receives:

- **`MAIN MIX`** (the default) is the Deluge's final stereo output: the same audio that goes to the main outputs,
  including any monitored inputs, but not the computer's own audio when it's mixed straight to the outputs.
- **`LINE IN`** is the Deluge's line input (or mic) as it arrives, before any of the Deluge's processing. This turns
  the Deluge into a simple audio interface for whatever's plugged into it. The internal mic only has one channel, so
  it's sent to both sides.

## Recording audio from your computer in an audio clip

USB can be chosen as the source for an audio clip, like the line input or another track:

1. In an audio clip, open the Audio Clip Sound Menu (:key[Shift + Select]).
2. Go to `AUDIO SOURCE` and select `USB INPUT` (`USB` on 7SEG).

USB is always a stereo source. Unlike the line input, it isn't normalized after recording, since the computer's audio
already arrives at the level it was meant to be.

Monitoring follows the audio output mode, as for any other source (see
[Select Audio Clip Source and Monitoring](/features/community_features#317---select-audio-clip-source-and-monitoring)):
`SAMPLER` and `LOOPER` monitor the USB input, while `PLAYER` doesn't.

<Aside type="caution">
  If an audio clip is monitoring the USB input, the computer's audio becomes
  part of the Deluge's output, which is sent back to the computer when `OUTPUT`
  is `MAIN MIX`. If your DAW is also playing its Deluge input back out to the
  Deluge, that makes a feedback loop. Turn off input monitoring in the DAW, or
  use the `PLAYER` mode on the clip.
</Aside>

Recording a sample from a synth or kit's sample menu still uses the line input only.

## Latency

Audio from the computer is buffered by about 6 ms (256 samples) on the Deluge before it's played or recorded, which
keeps it smooth when the Deluge is busy. Audio going to the computer is buffered by about 2 ms. Your computer adds its
own buffering on top of both, which you can usually set in your DAW.

## Multitrack mode

In multitrack mode the computer sees eight channels from the Deluge and four going back to it. Instead of the main mix,
the eight channels carry the tracks you route to them.

### Sending a track to the computer

1. Open the track's sound menu: in a synth clip, press :key[Select]; in an audio clip, :key[Select] or :key[Shift + Select]. In a kit, turn on `AFFECT ENTIRE` first,
   then press :key[Select] - a kit is routed as a whole, and the menu of a single drum has no USB routing.
2. Go to `USB AUDIO` (`USBA` on 7SEG) - the same name as the settings menu.
3. Turn on the channels you want. Any number can be on at once.

| Item                     | What it does                                                                     |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `CHANNEL 1` - `8`        | Sends the track to that channel, mixed down to mono                              |
| `CHANNELS 1-2` ... `7-8` | Sends the track to that pair in stereo: left on the first, right on the second   |
| `MAIN OUTPUTS` (`MAIN`)  | On by default. Off takes the track out of the Deluge's own outputs and headphones, whether or not a computer is attached |

- Routing belongs to the track, so launching another clip on it keeps it. It's saved with the song.
- Several tracks sent to the same channel are mixed together there.
- A new song sends nothing until you route a track.
- MIDI and CV tracks have no USB routing.
- Tracks are sent before the song's master effects and volume, and without their reverb, which stays in the Deluge's
  own outputs - even for a track taken out of them.

### Audio from the computer

The computer's channels 1-2 behave as in stereo mode: `INPUT` mixes them straight into the Deluge's outputs, and an
audio clip can use them as its source with `USB INPUT`. Channels 3-4 are a second source for audio clips,
`USB INPUT 3-4` (`U34` on 7SEG). They aren't mixed into the outputs on their own - use an audio clip to hear them. An
audio clip plays its input only in `LOOPER` mode, or `SAMPLER` while it's empty: hold the clip's pad and turn
:key[Select]. New audio clips start in `PLAYER`, which doesn't. Outside multitrack mode, a clip set to `USB INPUT 3-4` is
silent.

### Sending a track out through an effect and back

To process a track with an effect on your computer and hear it in time with the rest of the song:

1. Route the track to a pair, and turn its `MAIN OUTPUTS` off.
2. In your DAW, put the effect on that pair and send the result back to the Deluge's channels 3-4.
3. On the Deluge, set an audio clip's source to `USB INPUT 3-4` and play it.
4. Set `SETTINGS > USB AUDIO > DELAY COMPENSATION` to the round trip's delay through your computer, in ms.

Delay compensation holds the Deluge's own tracks back by that much, so the processed track lands in time. Audio clips
playing the computer's audio aren't held back, since it has already been out and back. With it on, everything else you
play on the Deluge is heard that much later, including the line input, but not the
metronome. Set it back to 0 when you don't need it.

With delay compensation on:

- An audio clip using `USB INPUT` or `USB INPUT 3-4` as its source isn't held back, even while it plays a recorded
  sample rather than the live input. Keep compensation at 0 while recording from the computer.
- That clip's reverb is still held back, so its reverb lands late against it.
- Recording the Deluge's own `MIX` records the held-back audio.

### Limitations

- Kits are routed as a whole, not per drum.
- 16 bits rather than 24, to fit eight channels into a full-speed USB connection.
- Windows hasn't been tried. Its built-in driver may not handle more than two channels.
- Each routed track costs a little processing, which can matter on a dense song.
